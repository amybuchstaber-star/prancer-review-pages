Different fibers.
Different jobs.

Unlike protein or carbs, human enzymes don’t break fiber down. That’s the magic. It travels through your system doing vital maintenance work along the way. One scoop covers the whole route.

  1. 1

    Clears the upper tract intact.

    Because your stomach enzymes don’t absorb fiber, it cruises past the stomach and small intestine ready to clock in where you actually need it.

  2. 2

    Gels up to keep things moving.

    Gel-forming soluble fiber (hello, psyllium) soaks up water along the route, giving your digestion reliable rhythm and form.

  3. 3

    Fuels your gut microbiome.

    Prebiotic fibers (resistant starch and acacia) arrive intact in the colon, feeding the beneficial bacteria that support gut resilience, recovery, and nutrient absorption.

Using it

How do I mix it? +

Drop one level scoop into 8–12 oz of cold water, give it a quick shake or brisk stir, and drink up! Want to switch up the texture? Stir a scoop into plain Greek yogurt. It turns it into a tart raspberry bowl.

When is the best time of day to take it? +

Whenever you can put it on autopilot. Most folks (Marcus included) take it first thing in the morning to knock out hydration and fiber in one 10-second habit.

Will this make me bloated or gassy? +

We specifically chose gentle, slow-fermenting prebiotics (acacia and resistant potato starch) paired with psyllium to avoid the digestive drama of cheap chicory/inulin bombs. Still, if your diet has been low in fiber, ease in with half a scoop for the first 5–7 days and drink plenty of water so your gut microbes can ramp up smoothly.

Can I put it in smoothies, oats, or yogurt? +

Cold water is the fastest move, and stirring it into Greek yogurt is a team favorite (Marcus does this multiple mornings a week). You can also blend it into a smoothie. Just drink it fairly soon after mixing since psyllium naturally gels over time!

Is it safe if I'm pregnant, nursing, or taking medication? +

Always check in with your doctor or pharmacist before adding any new supplement, especially if you're pregnant, nursing, or taking daily medications (since fiber can affect how fast certain meds absorb; spacing them out by an hour or two is standard advice, so ask your doc!). Check our full Health Disclaimer for details.
