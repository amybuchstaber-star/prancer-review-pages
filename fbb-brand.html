01

Positioning — the elevator pitch

This is the sentence that must be understood above the fold, in under 5 seconds. Everything else on the homepage exists to prove it.

The pitch (above the fold, verbatim)

Online training that builds a body that looks strong — and moves like it.

In 3 hours a week, coached by 6× CrossFit Games athlete Marcus Filly. No burnout, no beat-up joints, no 2-hour sessions.

Who it's for

Busy adults, mostly 35+, who've been burned by hype programs. They want visible muscle and a body that works pain-free — on a real schedule.

Why believe it

Marcus lived both ends: 6 straight CrossFit Games, then burnout — and rebuilt himself with this method. 10,000+ members, 4.9/5 from 2,400+ reviews.

The tagline

"Look Good. Move Well." is the promise. The pitch explains it; the method (3/60/10K) proves it; the reviews close it. Tagline never stands alone above the fold.

02

Voice — the coach, not the hype man

FBB sounds like a coach who has nothing to prove: direct, prescriptive, calm confidence. Short sentences. Specific numbers. Training vocabulary used naturally.

We say

  • "Three lifting days. Every body area twice." — prescriptive, specific
  • "Get built, not burnt." — the promise in five words
  • Tempo, sets, tracks, quality reps — real training language
  • "Costs less than one massage." — plain-spoken value

We never say

  • Crush / grind / beast mode / no excuses — hype-bro energy
  • "Get shredded in 30 days" — overclaiming
  • Exclamation points doing the work of evidence
  • More than one idea per sentence

Rule 1 — one orange per screen

At any scroll position, clay appears on exactly one element — the thing we want clicked or read. If two things are orange, neither is the CTA.

Rule 2 — aqua annotates

Aqua is Marcus's whiteboard marker: underlines, tags, small accents that point at things. It never fills large areas or buttons on FBB pages.

Rule 3 — dark = proof

Espresso sections are for evidence: results, the coach, video. Light sections are for pitch and offer. Alternating creates the page rhythm.

05

Graphic language — the coach's notebook

FBB's motifs come from how Marcus actually works: the whiteboard, the program sheet, the gym floor. Staple keeps the geometric blocks and halftones; FBB gets notation. Maximum one motif layer per section.

Marker stroke

The teal whiteboard underline. One per viewport, under the word that matters. Draws itself on scroll.

A1 · Tempo 30X1

Session tags

Real programming notation (A1/B1, tempo codes, Wk/Day) as annotation chips on photos and cards.

Grid paper

Faint programming-sheet grid behind light sections only. Never on dark sections, never with another motif.

06

Photography — real coach, real light

What we show

Marcus training, coaching, writing programming; members' honest transformations. Natural light, real gyms, the beach, the whiteboard. No stock, no studio muscle shots.

How photos sit

Framed like printouts pinned to the program: thin espresso border, slight rotation (±2°), soft shadow. Annotated with one session tag. No color-block backdrops — that's Staple's move.

Hierarchy

One hero image per section. Everything else supports at half scale or smaller. If a photo and a headline compete, the photo loses.

The fold — 5 seconds, 4 landings

Above the fold there are exactly four things for the eye to land on, in order — and nothing else. Every extra element we add competes with the pitch.

What got cut

Ticker bar, floating tags, coach's-note scribbles, marquee rows — all of it moves below the fold or dies. The fold is the pitch, full stop.

The 5-second test

A stranger must answer: what is it, who's it by, what do I do next. If any answer takes scrolling, the fold has failed.

One focal per section

This rule extends down the whole page: each section gets one headline, one visual, one action. Motion may only decorate the focal — never a side element.

The scroll is a pitch meeting: hook → problem → method → proof → offer → coach → family → capture. Light/dark alternation carries the rhythm; each beat earns the next.

09

Motion — earned, not ambient

Motion means coaching

Things move when they demonstrate or point: the marker stroke drawing under a word, numbers counting up, video playing. Decorative drift is capped at one element per section.

Video is proof

Click-to-play everywhere (never autoplay with sound). Hero video, member testimonials, movement demos — each appears at its beat in the story, not scattered.

Reveals stay subtle

Sections fade up once, 500ms, small distance. Respect reduced-motion. If a reveal makes the reader wait for the pitch, cut it.

10

CTA system — one path, three echoes

The whole site funnels to one action: start the free 14-day Persist trial. It appears at beats 1, 5 and 8 — always clay, always the same words. Everything else is a quiet underline link.

Try Persist Free for 14 Days ▸ Find your program — 60-sec quiz ← primary / secondary — never two primaries in view

Primary

Clay parallelogram, mono type, identical wording everywhere: "Try Persist Free for 14 Days." Repetition builds the path.

Secondary

Aqua-underlined text link. For the unsure: the quiz, member stories, Marcus's story. Never a filled button.

Tertiary

E-books and Coaching get their own buttons only inside the offer section, in aqua/espresso — they never compete with clay elsewhere.
