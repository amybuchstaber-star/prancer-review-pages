Why animal protein

The science is on
the side of the scoop.

We don't ask you to take our word for anything. Every claim below carries its source.

01

Complete, by nature

Your body needs nine essential amino acids it cannot make. Beef and egg white protein deliver all nine, in ratios your body can actually use. Most plant proteins have to blend 10–20 ingredients to approximate what animal protein does on its own.

Source: FAO — Dietary Protein Quality Evaluation in Human Nutrition, 2013
02

Absorbed, not just eaten

Protein only counts if your body can use it. DIAAS is the standard scientists use to score protein quality, and by that measure animal proteins consistently outrank plant proteins. Egg protein has long been the benchmark others are measured against.

Source: Burd et al., Sports Medicine, 2019 · FAO DIAAS Report
03

Enough leucine to matter

Muscle protein synthesis, the process your body uses to build and repair muscle, is triggered by the amino acid leucine. Research puts the threshold at roughly 2–3g per serving. A full 26g scoop of complete animal protein clears it. A half-measure of incomplete protein doesn't.

Source: Phillips & Van Loon, Journal of Sports Sciences · Nutrition & Metabolism, 2016
04

Muscle you keep

Muscle you don't feed is muscle you lose, and protein intake is one of the few things shown to help you keep it, alongside staying active. Higher daily protein supports lean mass, strength, and recovery through every season of training and life.

Source: Morton et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2018

We built Paleo Pro on a simple bet: real animal protein beats engineered blends. Here is the evidence, in plain language, with every source named. If we can't cite it, we don't say it.

Protein quality, scored

Not all protein counts the same

DIAAS is the score nutrition scientists use to grade protein quality — how complete it is, and how much your body actually absorbs. A score near or above 1.0 means a complete, highly usable protein.

The leucine threshold

Muscle protein synthesis, the process your body uses to build and repair muscle, is switched on by the amino acid leucine. Research puts the trigger at roughly 2–3g per serving. A full 26g scoop of complete animal protein clears that bar. A small serving of incomplete protein never flips the switch.

Sources 3 & 4 below

Muscle you keep

Muscle you don't feed is muscle you lose, and protein intake is one of the few levers proven to help you keep it, alongside staying active. Higher daily protein supports lean mass, strength, and recovery through every season of training and life.

Source 5 below

Easy on the gut, on purpose

No whey means no lactose. No soy, no gluten, no gums, no sugar alcohols — the usual suspects behind protein-shake bloat simply are not in the bag. Beef and egg proteins digest cleanly, which is why people who quit whey tend to stay here.

Sweetness from a fruit

Monk fruit has been used as a sweetener for centuries and carries zero sugar and zero calories into the scoop. No artificial sweeteners, no cane sugar, no blood-sugar rollercoaster.
