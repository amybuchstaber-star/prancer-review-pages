The tub in the back of the pantry

Most people don't decide to stop using whey. They just stop reaching for it. The tub moves from the counter to the cupboard, then to the back of the cupboard, and one day there's a half-full container of something that used to be part of the morning and now isn't.

When customers tell us why, it's rarely dramatic. The shake started sitting differently than it used to. The label got read more carefully. Or a doctor, a daughter, or a nutritionist used the word "dairy" and the tub never quite recovered from it.

Whatever the reason, the question that follows is the same: if not whey, then what? Because the numbers were the whole point. Nobody wants to trade 26 grams of protein for a plant powder that tastes like the bag it came in.

What whey actually is

It's worth saying plainly, because the tub never does. Whey is milk. When milk is made into cheese, it separates into curds and a watery liquid — the whey. That liquid gets filtered and dried into the powder. "Whey isolate," "whey concentrate," "hydrolyzed whey" — different levels of filtering, same starting point.

So if you're leaving dairy, you're leaving whey. There isn't a version of it that gets around that. Which leaves two roads: plant protein, or another animal protein that was never milk to begin with.

The question Doug asked in 2013

Paleo Pro started with the second road. In 2013 our founder, Doug, was rebuilding his body after an accident that had left him unable to walk. He was doing it with real food — meat, eggs, vegetables — and he went looking for a protein powder that matched the plate.

Everything on the shelf was whey, soy, or a 25-ingredient lab project. So he made the thing he couldn't buy, in his own kitchen, with one rule: real animal protein, nothing else. Grass-fed beef protein isolate for the bulk of it. Egg white protein alongside. No milk anywhere in the process.

That recipe is still the recipe. The pouch has changed; the two ingredients on the front of it haven't.
